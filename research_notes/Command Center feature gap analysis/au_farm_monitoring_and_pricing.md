## (a) What Australian farm/water monitoring products offer (feature baseline a contractor portal should match)

### Takeaway
The AU farm-water market is led by single-purpose, battery/solar-powered sensors (tank, trough, dam, rain, pump) that send data over satellite, 4G/LTE-M/NB-IoT or LoRaWAN to a phone app. They alert by SMS and email on low, high or fast-falling levels and sell as hardware (~A$500-2,350 per device) plus an annual subscription (~A$135-360 per device per year). A contractor portal has to match "SMS on low water / rapid drop / pump fault" at minimum. It can stand out with multi-site control, covering pumps, compressors, doors and solar on one screen, which the sensor vendors mostly do not offer.

### Inferences
- Typical AU farm-water price point: **~A$500-1,500 hardware per sensor plus ~A$135-360 per sensor per year** (A$11-30 per month). Saturn's monthly fee will be compared against these per-sensor numbers, so pricing per site (with several assets included) rather than per sensor could be a clear differentiator.
- Features to match: low, high and rapid-drop tank alerts; trough level; rain gauge or rainfall history; pump run and fault status; SMS plus email plus push alerts; phone-friendly dashboard; solar or battery power on remote sensors; satellite option for paddocks with no coverage; usage and consumption trends. Integration with farm software (AgriWebb) is a nice-to-have.
- Gaps in the vendor products that Saturn can fill: they are mostly monitor-only and single-vendor. Few control pumps or compressors remotely, and none combine farm water, sheds, doors, lights and solar in one view. Local install and ongoing support is also a gap, and farmers explicitly value it (Wildeye case study).
- A property map view (asset pins on a satellite image) is common in AgTech dashboards, but I did not find a citable source for it in these products. See Gaps.

## (b) Connectivity realities for regional QLD and how platforms cope

### Takeaway
Connectivity is the number one barrier to farm tech in Australia. Vendors work around it by offering several radio options per product: satellite IoT (Myriota, Globalstar, Viasat/Inmarsat), Telstra LTE-M/NB-IoT, and LoRaWAN gateways backhauled over Starlink or 4G. A Saturn site gateway should assume intermittent WAN. That means dual-SIM 4G failover routers (e.g. Teltonika) with SMS alerts sent locally, plus local buffering of data.

### Cited Findings
- About two-thirds of grain growers face barriers to new tech. Internet connection is the most cited at 67%, then high cost at 30% and lack of access at 26% (GrainGrowers/NFF survey). — [Beef Central](https://www.beefcentral.com/ag-tech/connectivity-still-challenges-greater-technology-uptake-in-beef-industry/)
- An ACM Agri survey ranked the top three barriers as cost of implementation, lack of skills to operate and troubleshoot electronics, and lack of fast, reliable, affordable connectivity. — [Farm Online](https://www.farmonline.com.au/story/8552716/agtech-adoption-trends-in-australian-farming-revealed/?src=rss)
- In an ABARES survey of more than 2,200 farmers, remote farms using mobile or satellite internet were more likely to report inadequate internet as an impediment. — [ABARES ICT use](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/sites/default/files/sitecollectiondocuments/abares/ict-use-australian-agriculture.pdf)
- More than 60% of farmers did not know their on-farm connectivity options or who to ask (2018). — [GRDC](https://grdc.com.au/resources-and-publications/grdc-update-papers/tab-content/grdc-update-papers/2018/02/connecting-to-our-farming-future)
- A Queensland (QUT) study found a "data capability gap" between the devices companies supply and farmers' ability to use the data. — [QUT Rural Data Divides](https://eprints.qut.edu.au/239856/1/Marshall_RURAL_DATA_DIVIDES.pdf)
- Telstra LTE-M covers about 3 million sq km and is aimed at low-power devices in hard-to-reach places. Coverage maps are predictive outdoor estimates. — [Telstra IoT coverage map](https://www.telstra.com.au/business-enterprise/about-enterprise/our-network/iot-coverage-map)
- Telstra resells Starlink to rural business customers and has an enterprise Starlink product. — [Telstra Starlink Enterprise](https://www.telstra.com.au/business-enterprise/products/networks/satellite-services/satellite-data-services/starlink-enterprise-internet); [Farming Ahead](https://www.farmingahead.com.au/technology-and-innovation/news-articles/4395583/telstra-ramps-starlink-satellite-services)
- KPMG says 4G/5G cannot be relied on across all farmland and names LoRaWAN as relevant for paddock IoT such as trough monitors. — [KPMG Agri 4.0](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/au/pdf/2019/agri-4-0-connectivity-digital-innovation-australian-farming.pdf)
- An MLA project (P.PSH.1182) connected remote Queensland properties to central management using Starlink and 4G boosters. — [MLA final report](https://www.mla.com.au/contentassets/33ffb8eda2b44c838b7e3c8fd1103a2a/p.psh.1182-final-report.pdf)
- The federal On Farm Connectivity Program funded connectivity for a central QLD cattle farm (case study). — [Infrastructure.gov.au OFCP case study](https://www.infrastructure.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/ofcp-cs-rnd1-central-qld-cattle-farm.pdf)
- UNE SMART Farm is researching low-cost tank monitoring that uses satellites to read sensor data directly. — [UNE](https://www.une.edu.au/about-une/faculty-of-science-agriculture-business-and-law/school-of-science-and-technology/research/smart-farm/smart-farm-partners-and-projects/low-cost-remote-water-tank-monitoring-using-satellites-to-directly-access-sensor-data)
- Teltonika routers sold in AU (RUT240, RUT241, RUT956, RUTX12) offer automatic WAN failover (wired, Wi-Fi or mobile), dual SIM on some models, SMS status reports, SMS-triggered reboots and queries, SMS-to-email, and the RMS cloud platform for alerts and remote config. An AU mining case study uses a RUT956 with dual-SIM failover for remote telemetry. — [PB Tech RUT241](https://www.pbtech.com/au/product/NETTTK1241/Teltonika-RUT241-4G-LTE-CAT4-N300-WiFi-4-Industria); [Umart RUT240](https://www.umart.com.au/product/teltonika-rut240-instant-lte-failover-industrial-4g-lte-router-65381); [Teltonika AU mines case](https://teltonika-networks.com/use-cases/industrial-automation/4g-router-for-tsf-safety-in-australian-mines)
- M2MOne (AU) sells managed failover and remote management/SCADA connectivity. — [M2MOne Managed Failover](https://m2mone.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/M2MOne_Managed_Failover_May24.pdf)

### Inferences
- Recommended Saturn architecture for farms: a local Home Assistant/OpenPLC brain that keeps running automations offline. Pair it with a dual-WAN uplink (Starlink or NBN as primary, 4G as failover). Add an independent SMS path (a router SMS gateway or 4G modem) so critical alarms (pump fault, tank critical) still go out if the cloud or portal is down. The portal should show "last seen" or staleness per site and alert when a site goes offline.
- For paddock assets beyond Wi-Fi, a LoRaWAN gateway at the homestead (backhauled over the farm's internet) is the cheapest way to cover multiple sensors. Satellite IoT sensors from third parties (e.g. Farmbot) could be brought in by API rather than competed with.
- The farmer skills gap and the "who do I ask" awareness gap argue for a local installer-plus-support model, which suits Saturn.

### Gaps
- No source confirmed store-and-forward behaviour in specific farm products or Teltonika firmware.
- No information found on Meshed's LoRaWAN coverage around Warwick or the Southern Downs, or on NBN fixed wireless quality there.

## (c) What Australians pay for remote monitoring and support, and how it is structured

### Takeaway
Comparable AU monthly prices: residential back-to-base alarm monitoring ~A$35-65 per month, commercial up to ~A$150. Solar monitoring software ~A$6-10 per month per device. Farm sensor subscriptions ~A$11-30 per month per device. Managed IT ~A$90-350 per user per month, with A$1,000-1,500 per month minimums for small offices. HVAC maintenance is ~A$300-800 per unit per year. These suggest a Saturn ladder of roughly **A$20-40 per month (residential/self-serve alerts), A$50-100 per month (farm/standard with proactive response) and A$150+ per month (light-commercial premium with SLA and included call-outs)**. Those tiers are an inference, not a market quote.

### Inferences
- **Structures that recur:** (1) hardware upfront plus a recurring fee per device or site; (2) contract-term discounts (Telstra's 24 vs 36 months, Solar Analytics' 1, 5 and 10 years); (3) add-ons per extra zone or device (Telstra +A$6.50-10 per month); (4) bundled support for a fixed period, then a care plan (SmartBoyz); (5) MSP tiers split by response scope (support-only, managed, 24/7) with minimum monthly fees.
- **Suggested Saturn Smart Controls ladder (inference, needs testing):**
  - *Basic / Monitor* ~A$20-35 per month per site: portal and app, SMS, email and push alerts, monthly health check, software and firmware updates, business-hours remote support. Comparable to alarm monitoring at A$35+ and solar at A$10.
  - *Farm / Standard* ~A$50-90 per month per site: Basic plus multiple assets (tanks, pumps, compressors), proactive alert triage (Saturn calls the customer), remote fixes, quarterly report, discounted call-out rate. Beats buying 3-4 Farmbot-type subscriptions (~A$30-90 per month total) because control and install are included.

  - Offer 12, 24 and 36-month terms with hardware either sold upfront or financed into the fee (Telstra model). Charge per site plus per extra asset rather than per sensor.

## (d) Customer pain points and what farmers and small businesses value

### Takeaway
Farmers value time saved on water runs, reliability in poor coverage, simple alerts, and a local supplier who supports both device and software. Barriers are connectivity, cost and lack of skills. A local electrician who installs, keeps it working and answers the phone is the natural fit.

### Cited Findings
- Top barriers are connectivity (67%), cost (30%) and access (26%). — [Beef Central](https://www.beefcentral.com/ag-tech/connectivity-still-challenges-greater-technology-uptake-in-beef-industry/)
- The skills gap is in operating and troubleshooting electronics. — [Farm Online](https://www.farmonline.com.au/story/8552716/agtech-adoption-trends-in-australian-farming-revealed/?src=rss)
- Farmers adopt ICT when perceived benefits exceed costs. — [ABARES](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/sites/default/files/sitecollectiondocuments/abares/ict-use-australian-agriculture.pdf)
- Farmer advice: "find a supplier that provides support for devices as well as the software". — [Agriculture Victoria](https://agriculture.vic.gov.au/support-and-resources/funds-grants-programs/on-farm-demonstrations-program/agtech-for-earlier-decisions)
- Agriculture Victoria case study "Smart remote monitoring: productivity and peace of mind" frames value as labour saving plus peace of mind. — [Agriculture Victoria case study](https://agriculture.vic.gov.au/about/agriculture-in-victoria/safety-health-and-wellbeing-on-farms/smart-remote-monitoring-productivity-and-peace-of-mind); see also [AgTech provides on-farm labour savings, Young case study](https://agriculture.vic.gov.au/support-and-resources/funds-grants-programs/on-farm-demonstrations-program/agtech-provides-on-farm-labour-savings/Case-Study_Young.pdf)
- An MLA-funded Farmbot digital project report exists for livestock water monitoring (V.DIG.2022). — [MLA](https://www.mla.com.au/contentassets/0e15746c51c64adaa343ac5cd50ebab4/v.dig.2022-farmbot-final-report.pdf)

### Inferences
- Lead the sales pitch with "fewer water runs, know before stock run dry, it still works when the internet drops, and Saturn installs and fixes it." Show ROI in hours and fuel saved per week.
- Portal priorities: a clear red/amber/green status per site, alert history, offline indicator, and one-tap call to Saturn for support.

### Gaps
- No quantified time or fuel savings for water checking in AU sources that I could retrieve (the Victorian case studies may hold numbers, but were not fetched).
- No small-business (non-farm) pain-point survey found.

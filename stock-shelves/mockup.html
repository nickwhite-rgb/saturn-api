Behind the screen

  1. Waiting. The camera looks for a face. Nothing is recorded until someone walks up.
  2. Sign in. Face matched on the Pi itself, no cloud. If it can't tell, they use a PIN or key tag.
  3. Pick the job. Today's jobs come from the AroFlo schedule for that person.
  4. Grab gear. Each bin's scale sees the weight drop and turns it into a count.
  5. Walk away. Signs out after 60 s still. Usage gets logged to the job and queued for AroFlo.

Project brief · draft 1 · 31 Oct 2026

Saturn Stock Shelves

A weighing shelf for the workshop that counts gear, knows who took it and for which job, and tells the office when to reorder. Written so it can be handed to a maker or developer.

Why build itHow it worksParts list Counting accuracyFacial recognitionAroFlo link Build stagesOpen questions

Why build it

  • Nobody counts stock by hand. The shelf always knows what's on it.
  • Reordering stops depending on Nick. Low stock lands on a reorder list for the office to action.
  • Materials get billed. Gear is logged against the job when it leaves the shelf, so it's not forgotten on the invoice.
  • Accountability without nagging. Every removal has a name and a job, or it gets flagged.

  • Layout: 5 shelves × 10 bins. At 100 mm wide plus gaps, 10 bins fit a standard 1200 mm shelf.
  • Counting: each item has a set unit weight. Count = (scale reading − empty bin weight) ÷ unit weight, rounded.
  • Wiring: one controller per shelf keeps it to 5 cables back to the Pi, not 50.
  • Offline-safe: the Pi keeps working if the internet drops and syncs later.
  • Web side: sits alongside the existing Saturn API (same Railway database that already pulls AroFlo purchase history, so unit costs come for free).

Check the bin size

A double GPO face is about 115 × 73 mm, so in a 100 × 100 bin it only fits on its side. That's roughly 9–12 per bin, fewer if they're boxed. Consider 150 mm wide bins for GPOs and switches and keep 100 mm for breakers and small stuff. The scales don't care if bin sizes are mixed.

Optional extra, about $150: an LED strip under each bin, so a search for "RCBO 20A" lights up the right bin.

Counting accuracy

  • Great for: anything over about 20 g each. GPOs (~110 g), switches (~65 g), breakers (~110–160 g), downlights (~210 g).
  • Not for single tiny items: screws, lugs, cable ties. Store those in bags or boxes and count the bag.
  • Temperature: a tin shed in Warwick swings from frost to 40°C, which makes load cells drift. The software re-zeroes when it knows a bin is empty, and a monthly 2-minute recalibrate keeps it honest.
  • Mixed bins don't work. One product per bin, always. That's the main rule for staff.

Facial recognition

Doable on a Raspberry Pi with open-source tools. Sign-in takes under a second. Things to get right:

  • Keep it local. Store face "templates" (a string of numbers), not photos, on the Pi only. Nothing goes to the cloud.
  • Always have a fallback. Caps, sunnies, masks and bad lighting will beat it sometimes. A PIN or NFC key tag on the keyring covers it.
  • Unattributed removals. If weight leaves a bin and nobody is signed in, take one snapshot and flag it on the dashboard. That's the anti-walk-off feature, and it's less creepy than recording all day.
Privacy: get advice before switching it on

Face data counts as biometric "sensitive information" under the Australian Privacy Act. The regulator has come down hard on retailers using facial recognition. Small-business and employee-record exemptions might apply to Saturn, but the law is being reformed. I'm not a lawyer, so run it past your accountant or an IR adviser. The safe setup either way:

  • Written consent from each staff member, with the PIN/tag option for anyone who says no
  • A sign on the rack saying the camera is used for sign-in
  • Delete someone's face data the day they leave
  • A line about it in your staff handbook / SOP

AroFlo link

  • Jobs in: pull each tech's scheduled jobs for today, so the screen offers the right ones.
  • Materials out: push what was taken onto the job as materials, so it ends up on the invoice.
  • Reorders: the reorder list becomes draft purchase orders per supplier for the office to approve.
  • Item list and costs: match each bin to its AroFlo inventory item and supplier part number.

To confirm: whether AroFlo's API lets us write materials and POs (reading is already working in the Saturn API). If not, it falls back to a CSV export the office imports.

Build stages

Stage 1
One-shelf prototype

10 bins, scales, a laptop screen. No camera, no AroFlo. Run it for a month and prove the counts stay accurate through hot and cold days. About $500 in parts.

Stage 2
Full rack + dashboard

All 50 bins, touchscreen, PIN/key tag sign-in, web dashboard, low-stock email to the office.

Stage 3
Face sign-in + AroFlo jobs

Camera, staff enrolment, consent forms, today's jobs pulled from AroFlo, unattributed-removal snapshots.

Stage 4
Close the loop

Materials posted to AroFlo jobs automatically, draft POs per supplier, monthly usage reports per tech and per job.

# Alarms, history and secure logins close Saturn's gap

Saturn Command Center already does two things most commercial platforms don't: an AI you can ask about each site, and a careful remote-forcing workflow (confirm, 60-minute auto-clear, logged). What it lacks is the "boring core" that every competitor treats as standard. That core is **alarms that reach a phone and escalate until someone acknowledges them**, **stored history with trend charts**, **alerts when a site drops offline**, **per-person logins with two-factor authentication** and **an audit trail the customer can see**. Remote PLC portals (Ewon Talk2M, IXON, Tosibox), cloud SCADA (Ignition, ThingsBoard), solar fleet portals (SMA, Fronius, Enphase, Solar Analytics) and Australian farm-water products (Farmbot, Goanna) all ship some version of these. For selling monitoring to farms and homes, alarms matter most, because they are what customers pay a monthly fee for. The 6-digit PIN with a 30-day cookie matters most for selling *remote control*: no comparable platform lets a short PIN alone start pumps or open doors, and Australian and US government guidance now asks for phishing-resistant two-factor login on remote access to equipment. On pricing, Australians already pay about **A$11–30 per device per month for farm-water sensors**, **A$35–45 per month for back-to-base alarm monitoring** and **about A$60–100 a year for solar monitoring**. A per-site ladder of roughly A$20–35 (Monitor), A$50–90 (Farm) and A$150+ (Commercial with response) would sit comfortably in that market. Many vendor prices below came from search snippets because vendor sites were blocked during research, so treat every price as indicative and check it before quoting a customer.

## Five standard features separate Command Center from the market

Across about 25 platforms, the same gaps recur. **Alarm management** is universal. Ignition's Alarm Notification module waits a set delay, notifies the on-call roster and escalates to a supervisor if nobody responds. Alarms can be acknowledged from a link in the SMS ([Ignition docs](https://docs.inductiveautomation.com/docs/8.3/ignition-modules/alarm-notification)). WIN-911 passes unacknowledged alarms to the next responder by severity and schedule ([SmartSights WIN-911](https://smartsights.com/products/win-911/)). On the farm side, Farmbot sends text and email alerts for low, high and *rapidly falling* water levels ([NSW DPI Farmbot listings](https://www.agtech.dpi.nsw.gov.au/collections/water-management/suppliers_farmbot-monitoring-solutions)). Solar Analytics gives installers a fleet alert inbox split into "New" and "Active", with an acknowledge button ([Solar Analytics alerts page](https://info.solaranalytics.com/solar-analytics-installers-resellers/fleet-dashboard-features/alerts-page/)).

**History and trending** is the second gap, and it's more urgent than it looks. Home Assistant keeps detailed state history for only **10 days by default**. Its long-term hourly statistics cover numeric sensors only, not switches or binary sensors ([Home Assistant docs](https://www.home-assistant.io/docs/configuration/long-term-statistics/)). Pump and compressor on/off history, which you need for run hours and short-cycling checks, quietly disappears after 10 days. Competitors keep it centrally: Ignition Edge stores 35 days locally when the link drops ([IA Edge pricing](https://inductiveautomation.com/pricing/edge)), and Talk2M Pro includes historical dashboards ([HMS Talk2M Pro FAQ](https://support.hms-networks.com/hc/en-us/articles/18482049979026-Talk2m-FAQ-About-the-Talk2m-pro-plan)).

The third gap is **customer organisations and roles**. ThingsBoard has separate customer hierarchies with their own branding ([ThingsBoard PE](https://thingsboard.io/products/thingsboard-pe/)). Fronius Solar.web separates owner, supervisor and guest roles, logs every change and lets the owner revoke the installer's rights ([Fronius FAQ](https://www.fronius.com/en/faq-online-tools/faq-solar-energy/solarweb)). That last model is the trust arrangement a farmer will want before letting Saturn force outputs on their property. The fourth gap is **audit trails**: IXON keeps device-level and company-wide audit logs ([IXON TOMs](https://www.ixon.cloud/technical-and-organizational-measures)), and Talk2M logs who connected and when ([Talk2M brochure](https://coevagi.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Ewon-Talk2M.pdf)). The fifth is **managing the edge device**: health and heartbeat, remote firmware and program handling, and offline alerts. Talk2M Pro does remote firmware management ([HMS Talk2M Pro](https://www.hms-networks.com/talk2m/talk2m-pro)), Ignition 8.3 added Git version control ([automation.com](https://www.automation.com/en-us/products/september-2025/inductive-automation-ignition-8-3)), and SMA alerts on lost communication between portal and system ([SMA manual](https://manuals.sma.de/Business-Systeme-PL/en-US/15213608971.html)).

None of the platforms researched offered AI Q&A per site as a mainstream feature. Saturn's time-limited, logged forcing resembles Ignition's alarm "shelving" (time-boxed and auto-expiring) ([Ignition alarming docs](https://docs.inductiveautomation.com/docs/8.3/platform/alarming)), so the same pattern carries straight over to alarms.

## Security and safety must come before selling remote control

The security gap carries more weight than the feature gap, because Command Center can switch real equipment on other people's property. Australia's cyber agency co-authored the 2024 *Principles of OT Cybersecurity*, which call for separating networks, controlling connections and limiting access ([ASD/ACSC](https://www.cyber.gov.au/business-government/secure-design/operational-technology-environments/principles-operational-technology-cyber-security)). The January 2026 *Secure connectivity principles* say remote access to OT must be "mediated, monitored and controlled" ([ASD/ACSC](https://www.cyber.gov.au/business-government/secure-design/operational-technology-environments/secure-connectivity-principles-for-operational-technology)). For small businesses, the ACSC lists turning on MFA as a top-three first step ([ACSC small business guide](https://cyber.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-01/ACSC_Small_business_cyber_security_guide_January_2025.pdf)) and recommends aiming for **Essential Eight Maturity Level One**. The 2023 update to that model pushed toward phishing-resistant MFA such as passkeys ([ASD](https://www.cyber.gov.au/resources-business-and-government/essential-cybersecurity/essential-eight/essential-eight-maturity-model-changes)). For session lengths, NIST's 2025 guidance suggests re-authenticating at least every 24 hours and locking after no more than an hour idle ([NIST SP 800-63B-4](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/aal)). A 30-day session is far outside that for an account that can force outputs. A long-lived, view-only "wallboard" mode is a reasonable exception.

At the sites, the main technical risk is **Modbus TCP, which has no authentication or encryption**: anyone on the network can send commands ([Trout Software](https://www.trout.software/blog/how-to-secure-modbus-tcp-best-practices-for-modern-ics-networks)). Keep the Pi-to-TM3 traffic on its own VLAN with no internet route. Make sure Home Assistant, the OpenPLC web page (port 8080) and port 502 are never reachable from the internet, and change OpenPLC's default login. These come from the research notes' recommendations rather than a single source, but they follow the government principles above. Treat HA long-lived tokens as master keys. Use a dedicated, limited HA user per site, keep the encryption key out of the database, and rotate tokens.

The legal picture for a small Queensland business is lighter than you might expect, but it isn't zero. The **Privacy Act's small-business exemption (turnover of $3M or less) was kept** in the 2024 amendments ([APH Bills Digest](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/bd/bd2425/25bd016)), although the Government has agreed in principle to remove it later ([IDCARE](https://www.idcare.org/learning-centre/fact-sheets/what-to-do-if-your-business-has-been-breached)). The **new statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy (in force June 2025) has no small-business exemption** ([Coleman Greig](https://colemangreig.com.au/insights/blogs/concerns-for-business-under-the-privacy-and-other-legislation-amendment-act-2024)), so door logs, presence data and camera feeds from homes deserve care. Australian hosting is not legally required: cross-border rules are about accountability, not location ([OAIC APP 8 guidelines](https://www.oaic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0036/256959/APP-Guidelines-Chapter-8-Cross-border-disclosure-of-personal-information-October-2025-v1.3.PDF)). Railway's closest region is Singapore; there's no Sydney option ([Railway regions](https://docs.railway.com/reference/deployment-regions)). Sending site data to Claude for the AI feature should be disclosed in a privacy policy, with personal details kept out of prompts. For subscriptions, **unfair contract terms now attract penalties**, and the ACCC is focusing on auto-renewal, cancellation and exit fees ([ACCC](https://accc.gov.au/media-release/accc-welcomes-new-penalties-and-expansion-of-the-unfair-contract-terms-laws)). Keep terms simple, give notice before price changes and offer a fair exit. Critical-infrastructure (SOCI) rules almost certainly don't apply to farm or residential sites, because they target defined asset classes such as licensed water utilities ([CISC](https://www.cisc.gov.au/legislation-regulation-and-compliance/soci-act-2018)).

Safety is where your trade knowledge matters most. The Electrical Safety Act 2002 (Qld) places duties on the "person in control of electrical equipment" (s 38) and overrides WHS law where both apply ([Electrical Safety Act](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/current/act-2002-042)). It's a reasonable inference, though not confirmed by a source, that Saturn as designer and remote operator holds such duties for remotely started loads. AS/NZS 4024.1204 (IEC 60204-1) requires a risk-assessed consideration of **warning signals before hazardous starts**. It also requires a lockable mode selector, or an access-code equivalent, for different operating modes, and a separate deliberate start action ([EN 60204-1 text](https://eclass.uniwa.gr/modules/document/file.php/IDPE391/%CE%A0%CE%A1%CE%9F%CE%A4%CE%A5%CE%A0%CE%91/EN%2060204-1-2018.pdf)). When equipment is in Local, remote commands must be blocked ([Machinery Safety 101](https://machinerysafety101.com/2022/06/29/local-control/)). In practice, that means a physical **Local/Off/Remote switch** on each controlled pump, compressor, gate or door, read by the PLC and shown in the app. Isolation and lockout must defeat remote commands in hardware. Add a beacon or siren delay before remote starts of hazardous loads, and allow remote stop at all times. The regulation was remade as the Electrical Safety Regulation 2026 from 1 September 2026 with no new obligations, but section numbers changed ([WorkSafe QLD](https://www.worksafe.qld.gov.au/laws-and-compliance/electrical-safety-laws/electrical-safety-regulation-2026)). The research found no Queensland regulator guidance specific to app-based remote starting, so this is worth a call to the Electrical Safety Office before writing customer contracts.

Connectivity is a reliability issue as well as a feature issue. Internet connection is the most-cited barrier to farm tech, named by 67% of grain growers surveyed ([Beef Central](https://www.beefcentral.com/ag-tech/connectivity-still-challenges-greater-technology-uptake-in-beef-industry/)). Design every farm site to keep running locally and buffer data when the link drops. Use NBN or Starlink as the main link with 4G failover; Teltonika routers sold in Australia handle dual-SIM failover and SMS alerts ([Teltonika AU case](https://teltonika-networks.com/use-cases/industrial-automation/4g-router-for-tsf-safety-in-australian-mines)). A local SMS path means critical alarms still go out if Railway is down. Never queue "start pump" commands to run late after an outage.

Three lessons stand out. First, **Fallon Solutions, a south-east Queensland electrical contractor, already sells monitoring at "$1.50 a day"**, which shows a local sparky can carry a recurring fee. Apart from security monitoring, though, the research found no Australian electrical contractor publishing a monitoring-and-automation retainer. That leaves the position open but means there is no direct price benchmark. Second, farmers explicitly want someone local who supports the device *and* the software. A Victorian case study's advice was to "find a supplier that provides support for devices as well as the software" ([Agriculture Victoria](https://agriculture.vic.gov.au/support-and-resources/funds-grants-programs/on-farm-demonstrations-program/agtech-for-earlier-decisions)), and one vendor (Arcoflex) already lets a qualified electrician install its system ([PIRSA Arcoflex](https://pir.sa.gov.au/research/agtech/find_solutions/products/arcoflex)). Third, customers resent subscription changes. Solar Analytics users pushed back when lifetime subscriptions were cut and converted to five years at the 3G shutdown ([SolarQuotes](https://www.solarquotes.com.au/blog/solar-analytics-lifetime-subscriptions/)). Avoid "lifetime" promises tied to hardware or mobile networks that will age out. Integrators also dislike recurring fees for remote access alone; one PLCTalk user disputed a GBP 1,000/year Ewon quote as too high ([PLCTalk](https://www.plctalk.net/forums/threads/e-w-o-n-free-no-longer-to-be-free.140424/)). So charge for outcomes (alerts, response, reports), not for the tunnel.

A workable ladder, which is my inference from these benchmarks and needs testing with real customers, looks like this. **Monitor** at about A$20–35 per site per month covers the portal, SMS/email/push alerts, offline alerts, updates and business-hours remote help. **Farm** at about A$50–90 per site per month covers several assets (tanks, pumps, compressors), Saturn triaging alarms and phoning the customer, remote fixes, a quarterly report and a discounted call-out rate. That compares well with three or four Farmbot-style subscriptions at A$30–90 a month, which include no control and no installer. **Commercial** at A$150–250+ per site per month adds a defined after-hours response, an SLA with service credits, an included call-out or annual service, and compliance records. Telstra's Business Protect and Solar Analytics both use common structures that suit this: 12-, 24- and 36-month terms, hardware either sold upfront or financed into the fee, and add-ons per extra asset ([Telstra CIS](https://www.telstra.com.au/help/critical-information-summaries/business/business-telephony/business-protect/telstra-monitoring-services-business-protect-plan)). Running costs are low by comparison. SMS runs a few cents a message, and a white-label platform such as ThingsBoard works out to roughly US$1.50–7.50 per site per month, so most of the fee pays for Saturn's time and response.

## Conclusion

Command Center's AI and forcing workflow are real differentiators, but they don't sell monthly subscriptions on their own; alarms, history and offline alerts do. Locking down logins and the safety layer is a precondition for that sale, because one incident with a remotely started pump would cost more than the whole service earns. The order that follows from the research is to build trust first (items 1–7), then make the service efficient and visibly valuable (customer orgs, reports, AroFlo jobs from run hours), and only then chase energy and solar extras, where free OEM portals already compete.

The bigger insight is that Saturn's real competitor isn't Ignition or IXON, which are built for integrators on six-figure projects. It's the single-purpose farm sensor sold online with no installer behind it. Saturn wins by being the local electrician who puts pumps, tanks, sheds, solar and doors on one screen, can actually switch them safely, and answers the phone. Every feature on this list should be judged by whether it strengthens that promise.
